HW 2

Question 1

  1. Show that the following probability model: \[ \begin{aligned} X \mid \Lambda = \lambda & \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda) \\ \Lambda & \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta) \end{aligned} \] where \[ P(X = i \mid \Lambda = \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^i}{i!}, f_\Lambda(\lambda) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha - 1}e^{-\beta \lambda} \] results in the marginal distribution for \(X\): \[ P(X = i) = \left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1}\right)^{i} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{i!} \]
  2. Suggest a simulation algorithm for generating draws from this distribution
  3. When sampling \((X, Y) \sim g_{X,Y}\) with \(X \in \R\), \(Y \in \R\), why is making a histogram of only \(X\) equivalent to integrating out \(Y\) (i.e. \(g_X(x) = \int_{\mathcal{Y}} g_{X,Y}(x,y) dy\)

Solution

  1. \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)i!} \int_0^\infty \lambda^{\alpha + i - 1} e^{-(\lambda + 1)} d\lambda & = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)i!} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{(\beta+1)^{\alpha+i}} \\ & = \left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1}\right)^{i} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{i!} \end{aligned} \]
  2. Simulate \(\lambda_i \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta), i = 1, \dots, S\). Then for each draw of \(\lambda_i\), simulate \(Y_i \mid \lambda_i\) from a Poisson\((\lambda_i)\)
  3. Lots of ways to answer this one. A more formal way is to look at the quantity: \[ \begin{aligned} P(X \in (a,b)) & \approx \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \ind{X_i \in (a,b)} \\ & = \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \ind{X_i \in (a,b), Y_i \in \mathcal{Y}} \\ & \overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to} \int_a^b \int_{-\infty}^\infty g_{X,Y}(x,y) dy dx \end{aligned} \]

Question 2

Suppose our target distribution is Gamma\((n,1)\) \[ f_X(x) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} x^{\alpha - 1}e^{-\beta x} \] and we use the accept-reject algorithm with an exponential proposal distribution with rate \(\lambda\): \[ g_X(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x} \]

  1. Find \(M\) such that \(f_X(x) / g_X(x) \leq M\) for all \(x\)
  2. Find the optimal \(\lambda\).
  3. What makes this \(\lambda\) useful for our algorithm

Question 3

  1. Suppose \(f_X(x) \equiv U(0,1)\), \(h(x) = e^x\) and \(g_X(x) \equiv U(0, M)\) for \(M > 1\). Can we use \(g_X\) as an importance density for \(f_X\)?
  2. Which value of \(M\) gives the lowest variance for our importance sampler?

Question 4

Suppose we have \[ (X_i, Y_i) \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} f_{X,Y}(x,y), i = 1, \dots, S \]

and a known density \(w(x)\).

Show that \[ \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \frac{f_{X,Y}(x^*,Y_i)w(X_i)}{f_{X,Y}(X_i,Y_i)} \to f_X(x^*) \]

Remember the law of large numbers!

Question 5

  1. Suppose your collaborators find the probability of a rare event is \(p=0.005\); they’ve devised a simulator to generate samples and they can classify if a sample is a “rare” event. Roughly what is the minimum number of samples you need to draw from the simulator in order to confirm this probability so that the MCSE is less than \(10^{-3}\)?
  2. Now suppose your collaborators decide they want a relative MCSE of \(10^{-2}\). In other words, they want MCSE / \(p\) to be less than \(10^{-2}\) What is the minimum number of draws needed to achieve this relative error?

Question 6

Suppose we have \(X_i \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} g_X, i = 1, \dots, S\) and we wish to estimate the following estimand: \[ \Delta = \Exp{f(X)} - \Exp{h(X)} \] and we know that \(\theta = \Exp{h(X)}\).

We propose two estimators, \(\hat{\Delta}_1\) and \(\hat{\Delta}_2\). \[ \hat{\Delta}_1 = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^S (f(X_i) - \theta) \] \[ \hat{\Delta}_2 = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^S (f(X_i) - h(X_i)) \]

Suppose that \(\Var{h(X_1)} = \Var{h(X_2)} = \sigma^2\)

  1. Under what conditions is \(\hat{\Delta}_2\) a more efficient estimator than \(\hat{\Delta}_1\)?
  2. Now suppose we propose a third estimator, \(\hat{\Delta}_3\): \[ \hat{\Delta}_3 = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^S (f(X_i) - h(X_i) - \beta_\text{opt}(h(X_i) - \theta)) \]
  3. What is \(\beta_\text{opt}\), or the \(\beta\) which minimizes the variance of \(f(X_1) - h(X_1) - \beta(h(X_1) - \theta)\)
  4. Under what conditions is \(\hat{\Delta}_3\) a better estimator than either \(\hat{\Delta}_1\) or \(\hat{\Delta}_2\)?

## Question 1

1. Show that the following probability model:
$$
\begin{aligned}
X \mid \Lambda = \lambda & \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda) \\
\Lambda & \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)
\end{aligned}
$$
where 
$$
P(X = i \mid \Lambda = \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^i}{i!}, f_\Lambda(\lambda) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha - 1}e^{-\beta \lambda}
$$
results in the marginal distribution for $X$:
$$
P(X = i) = \left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1}\right)^{i} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{i!}
$$
2. Suggest a simulation algorithm for generating draws from this distribution
3. When sampling $(X, Y) \sim g_{X,Y}$ with $X \in \R$, $Y \in \R$, why is making a histogram of only $X$ equivalent to integrating out $Y$ (i.e. $g_X(x) = \int_{\mathcal{Y}} g_{X,Y}(x,y) dy$

## Solution

1. 
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)i!} \int_0^\infty \lambda^{\alpha + i - 1} e^{-(\lambda + 1)} d\lambda & = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)i!} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{(\beta+1)^{\alpha+i}} \\
& = \left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1}\right)^{i} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + i)}{i!}
\end{aligned}
$$
2. Simulate $\lambda_i \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta), i = 1, \dots, S$. Then for each draw of $\lambda_i$, simulate $Y_i \mid \lambda_i$ from a Poisson$(\lambda_i)$
3. Lots of ways to answer this one. A more formal way is to look at the quantity:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \in (a,b)) & \approx \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \ind{X_i \in (a,b)} \\
& = \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \ind{X_i \in (a,b), Y_i \in \mathcal{Y}} \\
& \overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to} \int_a^b \int_{-\infty}^\infty g_{X,Y}(x,y) dy dx 
\end{aligned}
$$
